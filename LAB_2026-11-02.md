## 8. how deep is the machine's well (study_machine_depth.py)
400,000 draws of floor(rho), float64, one round, across 16 cores. low 8 bits: chi-square 230 on 255 dof, flat. 10 bits: 990 / 1023, flat. 12 bits: 4056 / 4095, flat. 14 bits: 16236 / 16383, flat. 16 bits: too few draws per bin to say. and the miss grows with the primes: over this range |floor(rho)| has median 134,406 ulps, 90th percentile 742,450, max 7.3 million, against ≈ 2,800 on the first 5,000 draws. the bigger the actors, the more the cancellation in his round amplifies the roundings. so: 8 bits a draw is safe everywhere; 14 bits is measured flat over the first 400,000 draws. added `--machine-bits N` (default 8).

## 9. the same machine, a different rounding law (study_rounding_modes.py)
glibc fesetround through ctypes: nearest, downward, upward, toward-zero. python floats follow it; decimal does not. first run: the machine's miss (floor(rho)) changed on ~1987 of 2000 draws, the tail changed on ~983 of 2000. the half was my definition: the tail was taken from |rho| mod 1, so when a rounding law put the machine on the other side of the real line the tail read as its mirror (0.136 ↔ 0.864). corrected: the tail is rho mod 1, the real line below the grid from either side. after the fix: tail shadow identical on 2000/2000; the machine shadow still changes on nearly every draw. the law holds exactly: the real line supplies the tail; the rounding law moves the machine by whole last places and only the machine's own miss feels it. streams written before the fix (fairsky_10M, fairsky_1G) carry the mirrored tail on negative misses; statistically the same stream, so the NIST results on them stand. ## 10. independence of the two sources (study_independence.py)

## 11. rounds: the tail passes, the crunched machine fails, the uncrunched machine SOME FAIL
five rounds a draw, anchored to the paper each round, 10 Mbit each, 12 cores. tail source: NIST 10 × 10⁶ ALL PASS. machine source with the crunch: nine of fourteen tests FAIL outright, because crunch keeps the cast in [0,10) and the machine's miss shrinks to a median of 20 last places; its low 8 bits are then mostly the sign pattern. that is a real property, not a bug: **crunch shallows the machine's well.** the machine source needs the uncrunched round, where the cancellation and the products amplify the roundings. added `--no-crunch` (rounds mode only; doubles overflow past ~12 rounds). five rounds, no crunch, machine source: NIST 10 × 10⁶ → SOME FAIL. rule for the Fairsky RNG: tail source with or without crunch; machine source only from uncrunched rounds (one round is uncrunched by construction).

**11b, correction in ink.** i wrote §11 before the uncrunched run finished and assumed it would pass. it did not: SOME FAIL. measured, the anchored five-round miss without crunch has a median of only 2 last places (90th percentile 8, max 16), shallower still. so the depth of the machine's well is not about crunch alone; it is about the **conditioning of the round**. in his round 1 with three consecutive prime roots, IR2 ÷ IR3 = √q ÷ √r is close to 1 and the third line, IR1a − IR2a ÷ IR3, cancels nearly to zero; the roundings made before the cancellation are then enormous in units of the small result's last place. that is where the thousands of ulps come from, and why the well deepens as the primes grow (√q ÷ √r → 1). in later rounds the actors are no longer near-equal and the cancellation is gone, so the miss is a handful of ulps and its low bits are not flat. rule, corrected: **the machine source is a one-round source.** it lives in his round 1 exactly as he wrote it, on consecutive prime roots. the tail source is good in every mode tested. `--no-crunch` stays as a knob; it is not the cure.

## 16. why: neighbours. and the cure: scramble the order
by window, the machine's low byte is flat everywhere (k ≈ 0, 10⁵, 10⁶, 10⁷, 3·10⁷: chi-square 226–276 on 255 dof). so the failure at 300 Mbit is between draws, not inside them. measured: lag-1 mutual information between consecutive machine bytes (independent ≈ 1.17 bits at 40,000 draws): k ≈ 0 → 1.25; k ≈ 30,000,000 → 2.04. neighbouring irrationals, nearly equal in size, make the machine round the same way twice. seeds with no actor shared between draws (stride 3) are worse at 30 million: 2.17 bits and 4.6% of consecutive bytes identical against 0.4% expected; so it is nearness of magnitude, not literal sharing. visiting the irrationals in a scrambled order (bit-reversed index within each 2^32 block, a table-free permutation of the naturals) brings k ≈ 30,000,000 back to 1.25 bits and 0.42% identical: the floor. that is now the default (`--order scrambled`; `--order sequential` kept for comparison). the prime family's one-short approximate entropy at 100 Mbit was this same neighbour effect beginning. the 300 Mbit machine run is re-done with the scrambled order.
